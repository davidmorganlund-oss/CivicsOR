How the Oregon Legislature works

Who's in it, when it meets, how a bill moves, and where your voice counts.

Who's in it

  • The Senate has 30 members, elected to four-year terms.
  • The House has 60 members, elected to two-year terms.
  • Each Senate district is made of two House districts, so every Oregonian has one state senator and one state representative. There are no term limits. Find yours.

When it meets

  • Long sessions start in January of odd-numbered years and can last up to 160 days. This is when the two-year state budget is written.
  • Short sessions start in February of even-numbered years and can last up to 35 days.
  • Between sessions, committees meet during Legislative Days, a few days at a time every couple of months, to study issues and hear reports. These hearings are often where next session's bills take shape. The Legislature page lists them.

How a bill moves

  1. A legislator or committee asks for a draft. Legislative Counsel writes it, often months before session.
  2. It's introduced and sent to a committee.
  3. Public hearing. The committee hears testimony. This is the main place for the public to weigh in.
  4. Work session. The committee discusses, amends and votes. It doesn't usually take public testimony in a work session. Many bills never get a work session, and that's how most bills die.
  5. Floor vote, then the same steps in the other chamber.
  6. The governor signs, vetoes, or lets it become law without a signature.

Session has deadlines: bills that don't get a committee vote by a set date in their first chamber usually can't move that session.

A few Oregon rules worth knowing

  • Quorum: each chamber needs two-thirds of its members present to do business. Since voters passed Measure 113 in 2022, a legislator with 10 unexcused absences in a session can't serve the next term.
  • Taxes: bills that raise revenue need a three-fifths vote in each chamber.
  • Emergency clause: a bill that declares an emergency takes effect right away, and voters can't refer it to the ballot.
