How to read a bill or a meeting agenda

Bill numbers, bold type and brackets, amendments, and the parts of an agenda that tell you when to show up.

Oregon bill numbers

  • HB and SB: House and Senate bills, which become law if passed and signed.
  • HJR and SJR: joint resolutions, used to send constitutional amendments to voters.
  • HJM and SJM: joint memorials, messages to Congress or the federal government.
  • HCR and SCR: concurrent resolutions, often to honor someone or set legislative rules.
  • LC: a legislative concept, the draft of a bill before it's introduced. Interim committees often discuss LCs months before session.

Reading the text

  • Bold type is new. When a bill changes an existing law, words in boldface are being added.
  • [Italic words in brackets] are being removed.
  • Start with the summary at the top. It's a plain description written by Legislative Counsel, and it's updated as the bill changes.
  • Check the version. "A-Engrossed" means the bill was amended once in committee, "B-Engrossed" twice, and so on. "Enrolled" is the final version that passed both chambers.
  • Read the measure analysis. On OLIS, each bill has a staff measure summary, and fiscal and revenue impact statements that estimate what it costs.
  • Amendments are numbered, like -1 and -2. A committee may adopt one, several or none.

Reading a local agenda

  • Consent agenda: routine items approved together in one vote, with no discussion unless a member pulls an item. Watch it; important things sometimes land here.
  • Public hearing: the item where the public gets to testify. Many hearings are required by law, such as budgets and land use decisions.
  • Action item: the body expects to vote.
  • Work session or study session: discussion with staff, usually without votes or public testimony. It shows where an issue is headed.
  • Executive session: closed to the public for a reason the law allows. No final decisions can be made there.
  • The packet: the staff reports and documents behind each item, usually posted with the agenda. The staff report's recommendation is often what passes.

Sources

  • Oregon Legislative Information System (OLIS)
  • ORS chapter 192, Public Meetings Law (Oregon Legislature)
