Voting in Oregon, including after a conviction

Oregon votes by mail. Here is how to register, return your ballot on time, and what the law says about voting with a criminal record.

Registering

  • You must be a U.S. citizen to register and vote. You also need to live in Oregon and be at least 16 to register. You get your first ballot for an election held on or after your 18th birthday. Green card holders and other noncitizens can't register or vote in Oregon.
  • Register by 21 days before an election. You can register online with an Oregon driver's license or ID, or by mail.
  • Many people are registered automatically when they get or renew an Oregon license or ID at the DMV, and get a card in the mail asking which party, if any, they want.
  • Update your registration when you move, so your ballot finds you.

Your ballot

Every registered voter gets a ballot in the mail about two and a half weeks before each election, along with a voters' pamphlet for state elections.

  • Return it at an official drop box by 8 p.m. on Election Day. This is the surest way.
  • Or mail it. Postage is prepaid. A mailed ballot counts if it's postmarked by Election Day and arrives within seven days. Postmarks have been lagging, so the Secretary of State advises mailing your ballot at least a week before Election Day, or asking for a hand postmark at the post office counter.
  • Sign the envelope. Your signature is checked against your registration. If there's a problem, your county will contact you and give you time to fix it.
  • Need help or a replacement ballot? Call your county elections office. Each county also has accessible voting options.

Voting with a criminal record

Oregon has one of the simpler rules in the country:

  • You can't vote only while you're incarcerated for a felony conviction.
  • Your right to vote comes back automatically when you're released, including on parole or post-prison supervision. You do need to register again.
  • You can vote if you're on probation, held in jail before trial, or serving a misdemeanor sentence in jail.
  • The same rule applies to federal convictions for Oregon residents.

If you're helping someone leaving prison, registering to vote can go on the reentry checklist alongside ID and benefits.
