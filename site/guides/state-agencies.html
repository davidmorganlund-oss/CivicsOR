Working with state agencies and commenting on rules

State agencies write the rules that carry out Oregon's laws, and they have to take public comment first.

How rules get made

When the Legislature passes a law, the agency in charge often writes administrative rules to carry it out. Oregon's Administrative Procedures Act (ORS chapter 183) sets the steps:

  • Notice. The agency publishes a notice of proposed rulemaking in the Oregon Bulletin, which comes out on the first business day of each month, at least 21 days before the rule takes effect. The notice explains the need for the rule, its cost and the deadline for comments.
  • Comments. Anyone can send written comments by the deadline.
  • Hearings. An agency holds a public hearing if it chooses to, or if ten or more people, or a group representing ten or more, ask for one in time.
  • Advisory committees. Many agencies form rulemaking advisory committees with members of the public before they propose a rule.

Getting notice

  • Each agency must keep a mailing list of people who ask to be told about its rulemaking. Ask its rules coordinator to add you.
  • Browse proposed rules in the Oregon Bulletin on the Secretary of State's website.

Writing a comment that counts

  • Name the rule and the section you're commenting on.
  • Explain how it would affect you, your work or your community, with specifics.
  • Suggest a change. Agencies have to respond to the substance of comments, not count them.

When you're stuck with an agency

  • Ask the agency's ombudsman or customer service office, if it has one.
  • Contact your state senator or representative. Their offices help constituents with state agencies. See who represents you.
  • Ask for records under the Public Records Law. See public records requests.

Sources
