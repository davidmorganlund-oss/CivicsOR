How Oregon's budgets are made, and where you can weigh in

The state writes a two-year budget, and every city, county, school district and special district writes its own, with members of the public at the table.

The state budget

  • Two years at a time. Oregon budgets for a biennium that runs from July 1 of an odd-numbered year. The Legislature adopts it during the long session.
  • How big. The 2025-27 budget the Legislature adopted in June 2025 totals about $139 billion, of which about $37 billion is General Fund, mostly from the personal income tax. The rest is lottery money, federal funds and fees.
  • Where it goes. Education is the largest use of General Fund and lottery money, followed by human services, public safety and health.
  • Who writes it. The governor proposes a budget in December before the long session. The Joint Committee on Ways and Means and its subcommittees hold hearings on each agency's budget, and the Legislature can adjust it in the short session.
  • The kicker. When personal income tax revenue comes in more than 2 percent above the forecast, the surplus goes back to taxpayers as a credit.

Weighing in on the state budget

  • Ways and Means holds public hearings around the state, usually in the spring of the long session. They're on the Legislature page when scheduled.
  • Testify or send written testimony on an agency's budget bill, just like any other bill.
  • Tell your own legislators what matters to you before the budget is final. See how to contact them.

Local budgets: a seat for the public

Oregon's Local Budget Law gives residents an unusually direct role. Every city, county, school district and special district has a budget committee made up of its elected board plus an equal number of residents appointed by that board. All members have an equal vote. The committee reviews the proposed budget in public meetings, takes public comment, and approves a budget before the governing body adopts it, usually in May and June.

  • Apply to serve. Openings for citizen members are posted by each local government, often in winter.
  • Comment. Budget committee meetings are open, and there's a public hearing before the budget is adopted.
