Serve on a board or commission

Hundreds of public boards in Oregon are made up of residents. You don't need to be an expert to apply, and many seats sit empty for lack of applicants.

State boards and commissions

The governor appoints members to more than 250 state boards and commissions, from licensing boards to the commissions that oversee major agencies. Many seats are set aside for members of the public, or for people from a particular region or background.

  • Apply online through the Governor's Boards and Commissions page. You create an account, search for the board, and apply. Include a résumé and a short statement of interest.
  • Some appointments need Senate confirmation. The Senate Committee on Rules reviews them during Legislative Days and the session, and the public can submit testimony.
  • Watch for openings. The governor's office posts lists of upcoming term expirations.

City, county and district boards

Cities and counties have their own advisory committees: planning commissions, budget committees, parks boards, library boards, equity and public safety committees, and more. Special districts and school districts have budget committees with resident members too. Look for "boards and commissions" or "volunteer opportunities" on your city or county website.

What to expect

  • Most boards meet monthly or less, and their meetings follow Oregon's Public Meetings Law. See public meetings.
  • Members may need to file an annual statement of economic interest with the Oregon Government Ethics Commission and avoid conflicts of interest.
  • Many boards don't pay, but some reimburse expenses or pay a per diem.
