How to make a public records request in Oregon

How to ask for emails, contracts and reports, what it can cost, the deadlines, and what to do if the answer is no.

Your right

Under Oregon's Public Records Law (ORS 192.311 to 192.478), anyone can ask to inspect or get a copy of a public record from a state agency, county, city, school district or special district. You don't have to say why you want it. A public record is any writing about the public's business, in any form: email, text messages, spreadsheets, video, maps and more.

How to ask

  1. Find the right agency and its records officer. Many agencies have an online request form.
  2. Put it in writing. The legal deadlines start when the agency receives a written request.
  3. Be specific. Name the records, the people or topics, and a date range. "Emails between the city manager and the parks director about the Riverside Park contract, Jan. 1 to March 31, 2026" gets a faster, cheaper answer than "all emails about the park."
  4. Ask for electronic copies if that works for you. They're usually cheaper.

Deadlines

  • Within 5 business days, the agency must acknowledge your written request, or finish its response.
  • Within 10 more business days, it must either finish its response or give you a written estimate of when it will, and of any fee.
  • The overall rule is to respond as soon as practicable and without unreasonable delay. Large requests can take longer; the agency should tell you why.

Costs

An agency can charge a reasonable fee to cover its actual costs of finding, reviewing and copying records, and it may ask you to pay before it starts. It must give you an estimate first if the fee will be more than $25. You can ask for a fee waiver or reduction if releasing the records is in the public interest, for example because they shed light on how government is working. Say why in your request.

If they say no

  • Ask which exemption they're relying on. Oregon law lists the kinds of records that can be withheld, such as some personnel and medical records and records about pending litigation. Often only part of a record can be withheld, and the rest must be released.
  • Petition for review. If a state agency denies your request, you can petition the Attorney General. If a local agency does, you can petition the district attorney of that county. Either can order the records released. You can also go to circuit court.
  • Get help. The Oregon Public Records Advocate helps resolve disputes over requests to state agencies, and to local bodies that agree to take part.
