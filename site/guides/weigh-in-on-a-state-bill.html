How to weigh in on a state bill

Four ways to tell the Oregon Legislature what you think, and the timing that catches people.

1. Testify at a public hearing, in person or remotely

  1. Find the bill on OLIS, the Legislature's website for bills and committees.
  2. Once the bill is scheduled for a public hearing, choose Register to Testify. You can't register before it's scheduled.
  3. Choose to testify in person at the Capitol in Salem, or remotely by video or phone. You'll get an email with how to join.
  4. Register early. Registration closes shortly before the meeting starts.
  5. Keep it short: who you are, where you live, and how the bill would affect you. Chairs often limit each person to two or three minutes.

2. Submit written testimony

On the bill's page in OLIS, choose Submit Testimony. You can submit from the time a bill is scheduled for a public hearing until 48 hours after the scheduled start of the hearing. Written testimony counts, and it's a good way to add detail you can't fit into two minutes.

Everything you submit is public and searchable online, including your name and anything in the document. Leave out your address, phone number and anything else you don't want posted.

3. Contact your own legislators

Email or call your senator and representative, especially before a committee vote or floor vote. See how to contact the people who represent you.

4. Follow the bill

Sign up for Capitol e-Subscribe to get email when a bill or a committee agenda changes. The Legislature page here shows what each committee is hearing.

Timing that catches people

  • Hearings are scheduled on short notice. Committee agendas often post just a few days ahead.
  • Public hearing versus work session. The public hearing is where you testify. By the work session, the committee is voting, so contact members before it.
  • The first-chamber deadline. Most bills that haven't had a committee vote by a set date early in session are done for the year.

Questions? The Legislature's information line is 1-800-332-2313.
