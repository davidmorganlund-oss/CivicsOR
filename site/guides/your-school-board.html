Your school board

Oregon's school boards are elected in low-turnout May elections, and they decide budgets, curriculum policy and who leads the district.

What a school board does

  • Hires and evaluates the superintendent.
  • Adopts the budget, with a budget committee that includes an equal number of residents appointed by the board.
  • Sets district policy, approves curriculum adoptions and labor contracts, and decides whether to ask voters for bonds or local option levies.

Day-to-day decisions about a school belong to the principal and district staff, so start there with a problem about one classroom or student.

Elections

  • Board members serve four-year terms, elected on a nonpartisan ballot in May of odd-numbered years. About half the seats are up each time.
  • The next school board election is May 18, 2027. Candidates file with the county elections office by March 18, 2027.
  • Turnout in these elections is often low, so each vote counts for more.

Taking part

  • Find the meetings. Boards usually meet once or twice a month. Agendas post a few days ahead, often on BoardDocs or the district's website. Many districts are on the Community Calendar.
  • Speak during public comment. Most boards set aside time and ask you to sign up first. See how to speak at a public meeting.
  • Join the budget committee or a district advisory committee.
  • Run. You need to be a registered voter living in the district, and some zones have their own residency rules. See running for office.
