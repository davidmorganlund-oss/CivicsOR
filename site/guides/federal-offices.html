Federal elections and who can run

Oregon sends eight people to Congress and helps elect a President. Here is who can run for each office and how often.

The offices

  • U.S. House of Representatives. Oregon has six House members, each elected from a congressional district for a two-year term. Every seat is on the ballot in every even-numbered year.
  • U.S. Senate. Oregon has two senators, elected statewide for six-year terms. Their terms are staggered, so the seats come up in different years.
  • President and Vice President. Elected every four years. Oregon's electoral votes go to the ticket that wins the state's popular vote.

Who can run (set by the U.S. Constitution)

  • House: at least 25 years old, a U.S. citizen for at least seven years, and a resident of the state. Members don't have to live in the district they represent.
  • Senate: at least 30 years old, a U.S. citizen for at least nine years, and a resident of the state.
  • President: at least 35 years old, a natural-born citizen, and a U.S. resident for at least 14 years. No one can be elected President more than twice.

How Oregon runs these elections

  • House and Senate nominees are chosen in Oregon's May primary. Major parties' primaries are closed: only voters registered with the party get its ballot. Nonaffiliated voters can still vote on nonpartisan races and measures.
  • Oregon's presidential primary is held at the same time, in May of presidential election years.
  • Candidates for Congress file with the Secretary of State and report campaign money to the Federal Election Commission.

How to follow your members

  • Find them with Who represents you.
  • Track their votes and bills on Congress.gov.
  • See who funds their campaigns at fec.gov.
