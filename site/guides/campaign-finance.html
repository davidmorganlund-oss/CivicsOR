Follow the money in Oregon campaigns

Every Oregon candidate and campaign reports its money to the Secretary of State, and anyone can look it up. Starting in 2027, Oregon also limits contributions for the first time.

Look it up in ORESTAR

ORESTAR is the Secretary of State's campaign finance system. Candidates and political committees file their contributions and expenditures there, for state and local races alike, wherever the candidate filed to run.

  • Search by candidate or committee name to see who gave, how much and when, and how the money was spent.
  • Search by contributor to see where a person or business has given.
  • Oregon uses continuous reporting, so transactions show up throughout the campaign, not just at a few deadlines.

Contribution limits start in 2027

For most of its history Oregon had no limits on campaign contributions. House Bill 4024 (2024) sets limits on contributions to candidates and committees, with enforcement starting January 1, 2027, and adds new disclosure requirements in later years. Local governments can set lower limits for their own elections, and some, like Portland and Multnomah County, already have them. The Secretary of State's Campaign Finance Manual has the current amounts.

Other places to look

  • Federal races (Congress and President) report to the Federal Election Commission at fec.gov, not ORESTAR.
  • Lobbying at the Legislature is registered and reported to the Oregon Government Ethics Commission.
  • Officials' finances. Many public officials file annual statements of economic interest with the Ethics Commission.

If you see a problem

You can file a complaint about a possible campaign finance violation with the Secretary of State's Elections Division.
