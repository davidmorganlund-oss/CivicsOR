Running for office in Oregon

Thousands of Oregon offices are on the ballot every two years, many with no one running. Here is how to file.

Which office, and when

  • State, county and city offices and Congress: the primary is in May of even-numbered years and the general election is in November. Partisan offices have party primaries; nonpartisan offices, such as judges and most county and city offices, may be decided in May if a candidate wins a majority.
  • School boards and special districts (fire, water, parks, ports, community colleges and more): elected in May of odd-numbered years. The next one is May 18, 2027.

How to file

  • Check the qualifications in the Secretary of State's candidate manuals: usually being a registered voter who lives in the district, sometimes for a set time. A partisan candidate must have been registered with the party 180 days before the filing deadline.
  • File with the right office. State and federal candidates file with the Secretary of State, online through ORESTAR. County, city and district candidates file with the county elections office or city recorder.
  • Pay the filing fee or gather signatures instead. Many local offices have no fee.
  • Watch the deadline. For the May 2026 primary, filing opened in September 2025 and closed in March 2026. Since 2026, elected incumbents running again must file a week earlier than everyone else; appointed incumbents use the regular deadline.

Money

  • Most candidates must set up a candidate committee and report contributions and spending in ORESTAR. See follow the money.
  • New contribution limits start January 1, 2027.

Getting on the voters' pamphlet

Candidates can file a statement and photo for the voters' pamphlet by paying a fee or gathering signatures, by a deadline after the filing deadline.
