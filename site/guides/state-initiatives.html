How Oregon's initiatives and referendums work

Oregon voters have been writing their own laws since 1902. Here is how a measure gets on the ballot.

Three ways onto the statewide ballot

  • Initiative: voters propose a new law or constitutional amendment by petition.
  • Referendum: voters petition to put a law the Legislature just passed on the ballot, so the people can approve or reject it. Laws passed with an emergency clause can't be referred this way.
  • Referral: the Legislature sends a measure to voters itself. Every constitutional amendment the Legislature proposes goes to the voters.

How many signatures

Signature counts are based on the total votes cast for governor at the last governor's election:

  • Initiative to change a law: 6 percent.
  • Initiative to amend the Oregon Constitution: 8 percent.
  • Referendum: 4 percent.

The governor's race in November 2026 resets these numbers for the 2028 cycle. The Secretary of State publishes the exact counts.

The steps for an initiative

  1. File a prospective petition with the Secretary of State, with the text of the measure.
  2. Get a ballot title. The Attorney General writes the short title and summary voters will see. Anyone can comment on the draft, and it can be appealed to the Oregon Supreme Court.
  3. Gather signatures from registered Oregon voters, following the state's rules for circulators.
  4. Turn them in no later than four months before the November general election, which means early July. County elections officials verify them.

What voters get

Every household gets a voters' pamphlet with each measure's text, an explanatory statement written by a committee that includes supporters and opponents, an estimate of its cost, and arguments for and against filed by anyone who pays the fee or gathers signatures.

Oregon also has a Citizens' Initiative Review. When it reviews a measure, a panel of randomly chosen voters spends several days hearing from both sides and experts, then writes a statement that goes in the voters' pamphlet. It doesn't review every measure.

Local measures

Counties, cities and districts have their own initiative and referendum process. For a county, an initiative takes signatures equal to 6 percent of the county's votes for governor, and a referendum 4 percent. Your county elections office can tell you the rules.
