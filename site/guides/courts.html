Oregon's courts, and where you fit in

Oregon's judges are elected, its courtrooms are open to the public, and jury service is one of the most direct ways to take part.

How the courts fit together

  • Circuit courts are where cases start: felonies and misdemeanors, lawsuits, divorce and custody, juvenile cases, small claims, landlord-tenant cases and more. There's a circuit court in every county, organized into 27 judicial districts.
  • The Oregon Tax Court hears cases about state tax laws, including property taxes.
  • The Court of Appeals hears most appeals from circuit courts and many state agency decisions. It has 13 judges.
  • The Oregon Supreme Court is the state's highest court. Its seven justices choose one of their own as Chief Justice, who also runs the state court system.
  • City municipal courts and county justice courts handle many traffic tickets, violations and some misdemeanors. They're run by local governments, not the state.

Judges are elected

All of Oregon's state judges, from circuit courts to the Supreme Court, are elected to six-year terms in nonpartisan elections. When a seat opens in the middle of a term, the governor appoints someone to fill it until the next general election, so many judges first reach the bench by appointment. Judicial races are on your May and November ballots; read the voters' pamphlet.

Ways to take part

  • Serve on a jury. If you get a summons, respond. Courts can often reschedule your service if the date doesn't work.
  • Watch. Most hearings and trials are open to the public. Oregon Supreme Court and Court of Appeals arguments are also streamed and recorded online.
  • Get help with your own case. Every courthouse has resources for people without a lawyer, and the Oregon Judicial Department's website has forms and guides. If you're charged with a crime and can't afford a lawyer, ask the court to appoint one.
  • Weigh in on the system. Court budgets, the number of judges and public defense funding are set by the Legislature. See how to weigh in on a state bill.
