Working with federal agencies

Federal agencies manage more than half the land in Oregon and run programs millions of Oregonians rely on. When one is stuck, slow or about to change a rule that affects you, there are ways through.

When you're stuck with an agency

Ask your member of Congress for help. Every House and Senate office has caseworkers who help constituents with federal agencies, including Social Security, Medicare, veterans' benefits, the IRS, passports and immigration cases.

  • Call or fill out the office's online form. Find your members with Who represents you.
  • Expect to sign a privacy release, which lets the agency share your case with the office.
  • Bring case numbers and copies of letters.

Public lands

The U.S. Forest Service and the Bureau of Land Management manage much of Oregon's forests and rangeland. Before big decisions, such as forest plans, timber sales, grazing permits and travel management, they usually take public comment under the National Environmental Policy Act. Sign up for the project lists of your local national forest or BLM district to hear about them.

Comment on a proposed rule

  • Find proposed rules in the Federal Register.
  • Comment on Regulations.gov before the deadline.
  • Be specific. Agencies have to consider the substance of comments, not count them. Explain how the rule would affect you, and suggest a change.

Request records

The Freedom of Information Act lets anyone request records from federal agencies. FOIA.gov explains how and links to each agency's request page.

Report a problem

Most agencies have an Inspector General who investigates fraud, waste and abuse and takes tips from the public, and many have an ombudsman who helps resolve complaints.
